Where to find a doctor, clinic or dentist on Koh Lanta, including 24-hour emergency care, plus nail studios and barbers.

## Doctors and clinics

You can walk in to all clinics.

- [Dr. Salarin](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dr+Salarin+Clinic+Phra+Ae+Koh+Lanta) - A good GP on Long Beach.
- [Dr. Pad in Saladan](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Dr+Pad+Clinic+Saladan+Koh+Lanta) - For something more advanced. Blood tests, ultrasound, X-ray and EKG on site, and they can send a doctor to your hotel. Open every day, with an emergency line and ambulance around the clock. Dr. Pad also offers the [Qdenga dengue vaccine](https://drpadclinic.com/product/dengue-vaccine-qdenga/) (two doses, three months apart).
- [Thonburi Lanta](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Thonburi+Lanta+Medical+Clinic+Koh+Lanta) - Outpatient clinic from 8:00 to 20:00, and a 24-hour emergency room with ambulance transfer. About 8 minutes from Saladan pier.
- [Koh Lanta Hospital](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Koh+Lanta+Hospital) - The public hospital, open 24 hours.

## Dentists

There are two dental clinics on the island:

- [Lanta Denté](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lanta+Dente+Koh+Lanta) - On Khlong Dao road. Check-ups and cleaning, root canals, implants, whitening and 3D X-ray. Open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00. See [lantadente.com](https://lantadente.com/).
- [Lanta Smile Dental Clinic](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Lanta+Smile+Dental+Clinic+Koh+Lanta) - The island's other dental clinic.

## How to avoid food poisoning

This is my personal, non-scientific approach for times when getting sick simply isn't an option. It is nearly impossible to judge a restaurant's hygiene just by looking at it — so the trick isn't finding the "safe" restaurant, it's reducing your overall exposure.

**Eat fewer meals.** Switching from three meals a day to two cuts your risk by roughly 33%. Simple math, real impact.

**Choose low-risk foods.** Meat, fish, and raw ingredients carry the highest risk. Opt for cooked or fried vegetarian dishes instead. If you're also worried about cooking oils, stick strictly to freshly cooked food and skip anything that's been sitting out.

**No salads.** Raw vegetables are an easy one to avoid.

**Skip the glass, drink from the can or bottle.** Ice cubes get a lot of attention online, but in Thailand this is generally a low concern — still, it's easy enough to avoid. More importantly, avoid drinking from glasses that may not have been properly washed.

**Have a Coke with your meal.** Not scientifically proven, but plausible: the phosphoric acid in Coca-Cola may help neutralize some bacteria. At worst, it's a refreshing precaution.

**Wash your hands with soap before every meal.** This one should go without saying, but it's easy to forget when you're settling into a restaurant and the food smells amazing.

**Use the [toilet at your accommodation](https://www.urbandictionary.com/define.php?term=Heimscheisser).** Public and restaurant toilets vary wildly in hygiene. If you can plan around it, do.
