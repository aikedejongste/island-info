The internet on Koh Lanta is fast enough for video calls. There's fiber along the west coast, the coworking spaces have backup lines, and mobile data works as a fallback. Power cuts happen, but they're short.

## Fiber and WiFi

The west coast has fiber internet from Saladan down to Klong Nin. Cafes average around 180 Mbps, and the coworking spaces have 1 Gbps lines (see [coworking](/coworking/) for details).

If you're booking a bungalow or apartment for work, ask the place for a recent speed test before you book. WiFi in accommodation varies a lot more than in the cafes.

## Backup when something goes down

The coworking spaces are the safest place for an important call:

- [KoHub](/coworking/#kohub) has three fiber connections from different providers. A UPS keeps the network running when the power goes, and a generator takes over after a few minutes. On the generator the fans, lights and power sockets work, but the AC stays off until mains power is back.
- [Monkey Business](/coworking/#monkey-business) has two 1 Gbps fiber lines with automatic failover, and is open year-round.
- [Fika](/coworking/#fika) has several fiber connections with backup lines.

## Power cuts

KoHub reports power cuts about once a month, usually for a couple of hours. If you work from your accommodation:

- Keep your laptop and phone charged, and carry a power bank.
- Keep mobile data on your phone as a backup, so you can hotspot when the WiFi drops.
- If you're staying for months and your work depends on it, a small UPS for your router keeps the WiFi up through short cuts.

## SIM cards and eSIMs

The two big mobile networks are AIS and True (dtac is now part of True). Coverage differs from place to place on the island. Around Long Beach, dtac has the best coverage in our experience. If your signal is weak at your bungalow, try the other network.

You have two options:

- A Thai tourist SIM or eSIM from AIS or True gives you a Thai phone number, which is handy for calling taxis and local businesses. Tourist packages cost roughly 400-1,200 THB for 8 to 30 days. Since 2025 the registration rules are stricter: you need your original passport and a live face check, so buy it at the counter at the airport when you land. Tourist SIMs last up to 60 days.
- A travel eSIM bought online before you fly is data only, with no Thai number, but you don't have to register it.

Staying longer than 60 days? You'll need a regular prepaid SIM from AIS or True, registered with your passport at one of their shops. For a broken phone on the island, see [phone repair](/repairs/).
